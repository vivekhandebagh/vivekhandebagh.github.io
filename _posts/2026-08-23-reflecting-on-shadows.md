> A camera is a device for learning how to see without a camera.
> —Dorothea Lang

This quote struck me with enough momentum for me to begin taking intentional photos more regularly. I now try taking at least one photo a day, and I try messing with only one parameter at a time. The goal for me here is not to necessarily to become a professional photographer, but to train my aesthetic intelligence. I've somewhat touched on why I feel this is important in [Beauty as Saving Grace]({% post_url 2026-01-25-beauty-as-saving-grace %}).

------------------------------------------------------------

Today was my first morning in [Keshavam](https://www.instagram.com/keshavamdc/) and I was completely enchanted. I must've spent at least an hour and half staring and taking photos. I think its because I'm not as accustomed to the Victorian ornamentation coming from the west coast. 

![one](/assets/shadow_images/IMG_5562.png)


However, as I kept trying to internalize the space, it slowly occurred to me that what I was being hypnotized by was the complexity of the shadows.

For the average non-artistically trained bum like me, I feel like shadows are initially a very non-obvious concept. Though in retrospect, its pretty simple and fundamental. If you try and imagine a sphere with no shadows whatsoever, you end up losing the 3rd dimension and the object flattens to a circle. Without shadows, volume remains as an abstract dimension. 

------------------------------------------------------------

I like this picture for what it demonstrates. 
![two](/assets/shadow_images/IMG_5544.png)

So much depth is created by the shadow here. 

But along with it, the shadow creates a profound sense of mystery. It's enigmatic and its unclear. There's so much space for letting the brain try and fill the details in (and we know from neuroscience that this is always what the brain is trying to do). In that sense, this photograph becomes deeply participative. And it mimics the way we actually go about the world.


--------------------------------

From here, it's only natural to recognize that there exists an interplay between the shadow and its dual. But I felt this more deeply than I probably should have. It feels like when the shadow is given respect, or darkness given a seat in your frame, the light starts to take form. 


As in this photo, the light feels like a character, as if it's actively walking through the door.

![three](/assets/shadow_images/IMG_5559.png)


![four](/assets/shadow_images/IMG_5563.png)


![five](/assets/shadow_images/staircase.png)


Now this is interesting because even though its just a different framing of the same scene, the role of light and shadow is flipped.


![six](/assets/shadow_images/IMG_5564.png)

 Usually, you have something like a spotlit object contrasted against a darker scape. But here the lightful object is much flatter, and the shadows are the depthful object placed over a light background instead.
