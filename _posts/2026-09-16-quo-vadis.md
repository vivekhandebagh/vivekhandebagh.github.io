There’s a scene in the apocryphal _Acts of Peter_. After the crucifixion of Christ, Peter has found his way out of Rome in escape from the persecution. He finds himself at the crossroads of _Via Appia_, when Christ appears in front of him.

> “Domine, quo vadis?”
> “Romam eo iterum crucifigi.”

> Lord, where are you going?
> I am going to Rome to be crucified again.

Peter realizes, and he turns back towards Rome and faces his martyrdom.

----------------------------------------

I had to shut my laptop and go for a walk outside. The words laid in my mind with a sense of heaviness. My lips kept murmuring "quo vadis?" in a rhythm that matched my gait. Where was I going? I didn't know.

---------------------------------------

There's an immediate, surface level reading of courage that I grasped and respected. But my intuition knew that there must be a deeper reading of this. I have enough meta-cognitive thoughts to realize that the weight with which these words struck me meant that there was some latent tension that it addressed. It took me an hour or two, but I think I have it.
